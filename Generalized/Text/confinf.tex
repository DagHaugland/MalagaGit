\section{Introduction} \label{intro}

Classic network flow models, like the \textsc{Minimum Cost Network Flow Problem} and the \textsc{Maximum Flow Problem},
enjoy favorable computational properties as well as a wide range of applications.
Imposing simple conditions on the flow pattern may, however,
transform a computationally tractable problem into a model of which only small instances can be solved in practical time.
\citet{chen06} introduce the concept of a \emph{confluent flow}, which is a network flow where each node has at most one flow-carrying leaving arc.
They prove that requiring the flow to be confluent, i.e., disallowing splitting of the flow at any node, may render otherwise well-solved flow models NP-hard.

Settings in which confluent flow makes sense are numerous.
According to \citet{chen06,chen07}, routing on the internet is a prominent example.
An immediate consequence of a flow being confluent is that all flow leaving a given node arrives at a unique sink,
which is consistent with destination-based internet routing.
Other examples include evacuation systems \citep{mamada05}, where the flow of people must be directed along a unique passageway.
Further, the confluent-flow condition applies to distribution networks where the distribution units must be organized in such a way that
all outbound transportation follows a unique link.

Extensions of confluent flow are straightforward to imagine.
A condition dual to the no-splitting rule is prohibition of merging.
By symmetry, it is plausible that optimizing network flow under the constraints that every node receives flow along a unique entering arc,
is as computationally demanding as the case of confluent flow.
In transportation and logistics, the said restriction has relevance whenever inbound flow cannot be distributed over multiple entering links.

Several well-studied flow models relate to confluent flow and extensions thereof.
The \textsc{Single-source Unsplittable Flow Problem} (UFP) \citep{kleinberg96,kolliopoulos02} asks for a unique path from a given source to each of a set of sinks.
A demand is associated with each sink, and flow must be assigned to the paths such that the demand is satisfied and arc capacities are respected.
As the flow paths constituting a UFP solution may intersect at one or more nodes, it does not hold in general that it is confluent.
However, the reverse implication applies, even in a multi-source extension of UFP: If a flow is confluent, then it follows a unique path between any source-sink pair.

Another related flow model is the \textsc{Disjoint Path Problem} (DPP) \citep{kolliopoulos07} in digraphs, in which pairs consisting of one source and one sink are given.
The problem is to find the largest subset of the pairs that can be joined by arc- or node-disjoint paths.
Feasible solutions are, in the node-disjoint version, necessarily confluent.
In contrast, two arc-disjoint paths may intersect at some node, in which case the solution is not confluent.
Conversly, a confluent flow does not necessarily induce a feasible solution to any of the two DPP variants.

In the current work, we conceptualize natural extensions of the confluent flow model.
Already indicated above, a flow involving no mixing of streams from different arcs entering any node is referred to as the \emph{influent} flow model.
That is, a flow is influent if the reverse of it is confluent.
We further introduce weaker flow conditions that relate strongly to confluent and influent flow, respectively.
A flow is said to be \emph{weakly confluent} if all the flow leaving a node arrives at a unique sink.
Obviously, if a flow is confluent in the strong sense, it is also confluent in the weak sense, whereas the reverse implication does not hold in general.
An analogous weak counterpart of influent flow is also introduced.
Weakly influent flow thus means that every node receives flow from at most one source, although the flow may be distributed across multiple entering arcs.

The weak fluency conditions are motivated, e.g., by logistics problems where each distribution unit is able to handle multiple routes along which the material
is leaving (entering) the unit, given that the entire flow is destined for (provenates from) a single recipient (supplier).
Another interesting flow model emerges when the distribution network may be composed of a mix of nodes,
some of which handle incoming flow from a single supplier and the rest handle flow to a single recipient.
In more abstract terms, the flow in a digraph is said to be \emph{weakly confluent-influent} if the arcs carrying positive flow induce a subgraph
where all source-sink paths intersecting any given node share either a common source or a common sink.
The strong counterpart of this condition is that either the in-degree or the out-degree of any node in the subgraph is at most one.

The support of a confluent (influent) flow is a forest of arborescences rooted at distinct sinks (sources), with the flow directed towards (from) the root.
When the conditions are satisfied only weakly, the support still has one connected component for each sink (in the weakly confluent case).
However, there may be multiple paths connecting a node and a sink.
Disregarding arc directions, the support of a confluent-influent flow may be a connected graph, even if the condition applies in the strong sense.

Since confluent flow models were introduced to the scientific community by \citet{chen06,chen07}, some attention to the topic has been given in the literature.
In their seminal article, \citet{chen06} analyze the problem of minimizing the congestion, defined as the largest flow/capacity ratio across arcs,
when the flow must be confluent and satisfy demand at all sinks.
Approximating the minimum congestion to within a factor 4/3 is proved to be NP-hard.
The follow-up work \citep{chen07} strengthens this result, and concludes that hardness persists if the approximation bound is relaxed to $1+\log_2{|T|}/2$,
where $T$ is the set of sinks in the graph.
\citet{dressler10} study the \textsc{Maximum Confluent Flow Problem}, for which they give complexity and approximability results.
In particular, the problem is proved to be NP-hard even for directed trees, but solvable in pseudo-polynomial time for graph classes of bounded treewidth.
The authors later prove that the single-sink version of the problem is solvable in polynomial time if the digraph is outerplanar \citep{dressler14}.

In the current letter, the following contributions are made: The extensions of confluent flow discussed above are conceptualized.
The complexity of the corresponding minimum cost flow problems is analyzed, and simple NP-hardness proofs are given.
With special focus on the weakly confluent-influent minimum cost flow problem, integer programming (single and multi-commodity flow) formulations are given.
In the context of these formulations, a host of redundant valid inequalities are given.

The remainder of the letter is organized as follows:
Mathematical notation and definitions of flow problems subject to fluency constraints are provided in Section \ref{sec:problem}.
Complexity results are summarized in Section~\ref{sec:compl}.
In Section~\ref{sec:sciMILP}, integer programming formulations are derived.
Finally, valid inequalities for the models are analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:vi}

\section{Mathematical notation and problem formulation}\label{sec:problem}

Let $D=(N,A)$ be a directed acyclic graph,
where the node set $N=S\cup P\cup T$ consists of the disjoint subsets $S$ of \emph{sources}, $P$ of \emph{internal nodes}, and $T$ of \emph{sinks}.
The sets of upstream and downstream neighbors of node $j\in N$ are denoted
$N_j^-=\left\{i\in N:(i,j)\in A\right\}$ and $N_j^-=\left\{i\in N:(j,i)\in A\right\}$, respectively.
The set of sources from which there exists a path to $j$ in $D$ is denoted $S_j$,
and $T_j$ is the set of sinks to which there is a path from $j$ in $D$.
Without relevant loss of generality, it is assumed that $S_j\neq\emptyset\neq T_j$ for every $j\in N$
(with the conventions that $s\in S_s$ and $t\in T_t$ for every $\in S$, $t\in T$),
and that $N_s^-=N_t^+=\emptyset$ for every $s\in S$ and $t\in T$.

Define the vectors of arc \emph{costs} $\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathbb{Q}^{A}$ and arc \emph{capacities} $\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{Q}_+^{A}$.
In the \textsc{Minimum Cost Flow Problem} (MCFP), the flow leaving a source and the flow entering a sink are subject to upper and lower bounds, respectively.
Denote by $b_s$ the \emph{supply} at source $s\in S$, let $b_t$ be the \emph{demand} at sink $t\in T$,
and let $\boldsymbol{b}=\left(b_u\right)_{u\in S\cup T}$.

For a flow vector $\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{R}_+^A$, denote the entering and leaving flow at node $i\in N$ by
$f_i^-=\sum_{j\in N_i^-}f_{ji}$ and $f_i^+=\sum_{j\in N_i^+}f_{ij}$, respectively. Define the \emph{flow polytope}
\[F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b}) = \left\{\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{R}_+^A:\boldsymbol{f}\leq\boldsymbol{h}, f_s^+\leq b_s ~(s\in S), f_p^+=f_p^- ~(p\in P), f_t^-\geq b_t ~(t\in T)\right\}.\]
For any $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$, let $D(\boldsymbol{f})=\left(N(\boldsymbol{f}), A(\boldsymbol{f})\right)$ denote the
\emph{support} of $\boldsymbol{f}$, i.e., the subgraph of $D$
where $A(\boldsymbol{f})\subseteq A$ consists of arcs $(i,j)\in A$ where $f_{ij}>0$, and
$N(\boldsymbol{f})\subseteq N$ consists of nodes in $N$ with at least one incident arc in $A(\boldsymbol{f})$.
Further, let $N_i^-(\boldsymbol{f})$ and $N_i^+(\boldsymbol{f})$ be the sets of in- and out-neighbors, respectively, of node $i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$.

\section{Confluent flow and extensions} \label{sec:conf}
In the current letter, the notion of a \emph{confluent} flow \cite{chen06} is pursued and extended.
First, confluency in the reverse flow direction is considered.
Second, in Section~\ref{sec:weak}, a relaxed version of confluent flow is introduced.
\subsection{Reversing the confluency condition} \label{sec:inf}
\begin{definition} \label{def:conf}
A flow $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$ is said to be \emph{confluent} if $|N_i^+(\boldsymbol{f})|\leq 1$ at all nodes $i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$.
% $f_{ij}>0$ for at most one $j\in N_i^+$.
\qed
\end{definition}

That is, confluence means that while flow streams are allowed to merge at any internal node, the flow has to leave along a unique arc.
Also all flow leaving a source must be allocated to the same arc. At the sinks $t\in T$, the condition is satisfied by the assumption $N_t^+=\emptyset$.
The inflow counterpart of confluent flow, where merging is disallowed at internal nodes and sinks, is defined analogously:
\begin{definition} \label{def:inf}
A flow $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$ is said to be \emph{influent} if $|N_i^-(\boldsymbol{f})|\leq 1$ at all nodes $i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$.
% A flow $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h})$ is said to be \emph{influent} if, at all nodes $i\in P\cup T$, $f_{ji}>0$ for at most one $j\in N_i^-$.
\qed
\end{definition}

By Definitions~\ref{def:conf}--\ref{def:inf}, confluent and influent flow are dual concepts, where the duality corresponds to arc reversals.
Combining the concepts disjunctively at each node introduces another fluency condition:
\begin{definition} \label{def:confinf}
A flow $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$ is said to be \emph{confluent-influent} if
$\min\left\{|N_i^-(\boldsymbol{f})|,|N_i^+(\boldsymbol{f})|\right\}\leq 1$ at all nodes $i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$.
% A flow $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h})$ is said to be \emph{confluent-influent} if, at all nodes $p\in P$, either $f_{pj}>0$ for at most one $j\in N_p^+$
% or $f_{jp}>0$ for at most one $j\in N_p^-$.
\qed
\end{definition}

Observe that by the assumption $N_s^-=\emptyset=N_t^+$ for all $s\in S$ and $t\in T$,
the condition in Definition~\ref{def:confinf} is satisfied at all sources and sinks.
Hence, in a bipartite digraph, where $P=\emptyset$, any feasible flow is confluent-influent.
In an arbitrary digraph, a flow that is either confluent or influent is necessarily confluent-influent, but the converse is not true.

The MCFP is extended by imposing any of the fluency conditions:
\begin{problem} \label{prob:conf}
The \textsc{Minimum Cost Confluent Flow Problem} (MCCFP):
Find a flow $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$ satisfying Definition~\ref{def:conf} such that the cost $\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}$ is minimized.
\qed
\end{problem}
\noindent
Analogously, in correspondence with Definitions~\ref{def:inf} and \ref{def:confinf}, respectively, the 
\textsc{Minimum Cost Influent Flow Problem} (MCIFP) and the \textsc{Minimum Cost Confluent-Influent Flow Problem} (MCCIFP) are defined.

\subsection{Weak fluency conditions} \label{sec:weak}

Different digraphs may be equivalent in the perspective of the MCFP.
Splitting any node into two new ones joined by an arc with sufficently large capacity and zero cost, renders the MCFP instance unchanged.
In contrast, the operation largely alters the MCCIFP instance, since any flow in the modified network is confluent-influent.
A concept that is more resilient to likely graph alterations is introduced next.

\begin{definition} \label{def:weakconfinf}
A flow $\boldsymbol{f}$ in $D$ is said to be \emph{weakly confluent} if, for all $i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$,
$D(\boldsymbol{f})$ has paths from $i$ to at most one sink $t\in T$.
If $D(\boldsymbol{f})$ has paths to $i$ from at most one source $s\in S$, then $\boldsymbol{f}$ is said to be \emph{weakly influent}.
If at least one of the conditions holds at all $i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$, then $\boldsymbol{f}$ is \emph{weakly confluent-influent}.
\qed
\end{definition}

Each weak fluency condition introduced in Definition~\ref{def:weakconfinf} is implied by its strong counterpart.
In bipartite graphs, weak confluency or influency implies the corresponding condition in the strong sense.
If all paths in $D$ have length at most 2, i.e., if $N_p^-\subseteq S$ and $N_p^+\subseteq T$ for all $p\in P$, then any weakly confluent-influent flow is also
confluent-influent.

\section{Complexity of fluency-constrained extensions of MCFP} \label{sec:compl}
A modified version of MCCFP, in which the congestion $\max_{(i,j)\in A}f_{ij}/h_{ij}$ is to be minimized, is known to be NP-hard \cite{chen06}, and even NP-hard to approximate within a factor
$\tfrac{4}{3}$.
\emph{Add more recent results!}
By a simple reduction from the \textsc{Subset Sum Problem},
it is next shown that MCCFP (Problem~\ref{prob:conf}) is NP-hard even in a restricted class of bipartite graphs.
\begin{proposition} \label{prop:conf}
MCCFP is NP-hard in instances where $D$ is bipartite, $P=\emptyset$, and $|T|=2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We prove that checking feaibility of MCCFP is NP-complete.

Consider an instance $(b_1,\ldots,b_n,d)$ of the \textsc{Subset Sum Problem}, with positive integers $1\leq b_1\leq\cdots\leq b_n\leq d$.
Let $D$ be the complete bipartite digraph with node sets $S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_n\}$ and $T=\{t_0,t_1\}$.
Let the supply at $s_i$ be $b_i$ ($i=1,\ldots,n$), while the demand at $t_0$ and $t_1$ is, respectively, $\sum_{i=1}^nb_i-d$ and $d$.
Both arcs leaving $s_i$ have capacity $b_i$ and arbitrary cost.
Because total supply equals total demand, $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$ only if $b_i$ flow units leave source $s_i$,
$d$ units enter sink $t_1$, and $\sum_{i=1}^nb_i-d$ units enter sink $t_0$.
Confluence implies that all the flow leaving $s_i$ enters exactly one of the sinks $t_0$ or $t_1$.
Thus, the MCCFP instance $(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})$ is feasible if and only if there exists a set $S_1\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ such that $\sum_{i\in S_1}b_i=d$.
\end{proof}

NP-hardness in restricted instance classes also applies to the influent and confluent-influent counterparts of MCCFP.
In the influent case, the proof repeats the arguments in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:conf} applied to a digraph with all arcs reversed.
\begin{proposition} \label{prop:inf}
MCIFP is NP-hard in instances where $D$ is bipartite, $P=\emptyset$, and $|S|=2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Consider the instance of the \textsc{Subset Sum Problem} introduced in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:conf}.
Let $D$ be the complete bipartite digraph with node sets $S=\{s_0,s_1\}$ and $T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_n\}$.
Let the demand at $t_i$ be $b_i$ ($i=1,\ldots,n$), while the supply at $s_0$ and $s_1$ is, respectively, $\sum_{i=1}^nb_i-d$ and $d$.
Finally, $c_{st_i}$ is arbitrary and $h_{st_i}=b_i$ for $s\in\{s_0,s_1\}$.
Following the arguments in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:conf}, $(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})$ is a feasible MCIFP instance
if and only if $\sum_{i\in S_1}b_i=d$ for some $S_1\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:conf} also applies if MCCFP is defined with the demand as upper bounds
(if the constraints $f_t^-\geq b_t$ are replaced by $f_t^-\leq b_t$).
Consider the digraph introduced in the proof of the proposition, and let the cost $c_{st}=-2$ for all $s=s_1,\ldots,s_n$ and $t=t_1$.
For $t=t_0$, let $c_{st}=-1$.
Hence, the minimum cost is $-t-\sum_{i=1}^nb_i$ if and only if $\sum_{i\in S_1}b_i=d$ for some $S_1\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
Analogously, Proposition~\ref{prop:inf} also holds if the definition of MCIFP undergoes the same modification.

% Let $\len{D}$ denote the maximum length of paths from a source to a sink in $D$.
While MCCIFP is solvcable in polynomial time in bipartite digraphs consisting only of sources and sinks,
introducing an intermediate set $P$ of nodes renders MCCIFP NP-hard, even if there are no arcs between any two nodes in $P$.
\begin{proposition} \label{prop:confinf}
MCCIFP is NP-hard in instances where $N_p^-\subseteq S$ and $N_p^+\subseteq T$ for all $p\in P$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Consider the instance $(b_1,\ldots,b_n,d)$ of the \textsc{Subset Sum Problem} introduced in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:conf}.
Let $D$ be the complete bipartite digraph with node sets $S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_n,s_1',\ldots,s_n'\}$, $P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}$, and $T=\{t_0,t_1\}$.
Let all arcs of $D$ be incident to $P$, where $N_{p_i}^-=\{s_i,s_i'\}$ and  $N_{p_i}^+=\{t_0,t_1\}$.
% Node $p_i\in P$ has entering arcs $(s_i,p_i)$ and $(s_i',p_i)$, and leaving arcs $(p_i,t_0)$
Let the supply at $s_i$ and $s_i'$ be $b_i$ ($i=1,\ldots,n$), while the demand at $t_0$ and $t_1$ is, respectively, $2\sum_{i=1}^nb_i-2d$ and $2d$.
Both arcs entering $p_i$ have capacity $b_i$, whereas both the leaving arcs have capacity $2b_i$. Arc costs are arbitrary.

Consider a feasible flow $\boldsymbol{f}$ in the MCCIFP instance $(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})$ hence defined.
Then, since total supply equals both the total capacity of arcs leaving $S$ and total demand, $f_{sp_i}=h_{sp_i}=b_i>0$ for all $s=s_i,s_i'$ and $i=1,\ldots,n$.
It follows that $\boldsymbol{f}$ is confluent, as it is not influent at any $p_i$.
Thus, all $2b_i$ units of flow leaving $p_i$ are sent to the same sink, $t_0$ or $t_1$.
Since $t_1$ receives $2d$ units of flow, there is a set $I_1\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}$ such that $\sum_{i\in I_1}f_{p_it_1}=2\sum_{i\in I_1}{b_i}=2d$.

Conversely, if $\sum_{i\in I_1}b_i=d$ for some $I_1\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, then the flow $\boldsymbol{f}$, where $f_{s_ip_i}=f_{s_i'p_i}=b_i$ ($i=1,\ldots,n\}$),
$f_{p_it_0}=\left\{\begin{array}{lr}2b_i, & i\notin I_1 \\ 0, & i\in I_1,\end{array}\right.$ and
$f_{p_it_1}=\left\{\begin{array}{lr}2b_i, & i\in I_1 \\ 0, & i\notin I_1,\end{array}\right.$ is feasible in the MCCIFP instance.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Extensions to weak fluency conditions} \label{sec:wcompl}
% It is readily observed that if $\boldsymbol{f}$ is confluent, then it is also weakly confluent.
% Analogous claims hold for the other two fluency conditions.
% Further, in bipartite digraphs, weak confluence (weak influence) implies confluence (influence).
% Finally, if $\len{D}=2$ (see the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:confinf}), then a weakly confluent-influent flow in $D$ is also confluent-influent.

weak counterpart, it follows immediately that the propositions apply even if the fluency condition is to be understood in the weak sense.

\section{Single-commodity flow formulations for weakly confluent-influent flow} \label{sec:sciMILP}
Combining the single-commodity flow variables $\boldsymbol{f}$ with binary variables gives a natural formulation of the weak MCCIFP.
For all $p\in P$, $s\in S_p$, and $t\in T_p$, let $x_p^s=1$ signify that all paths from $S$ to $p$ in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$ start at $s$.
Analogously, $x_p^t=1$ means that all paths from $p$ to $T$ terminate at $t$.
For notational convenience, define the constants $x_u^v=\left\{\begin{array}{lr}1, & u=v \\ 0, & u\neq v,\end{array}\right.$
for all $u,v\in S\cup T$, and $x_p^u=0$ for all $p\in P$ and $u\in (S\setminus S_p)\cup(T\setminus T_p)$.

Weakly confluent-influent flow dictates $\sum_{s\in S_p}x_p^s+\sum_{t\in T_p}x_p^t=1$ at all $p\in P$.
Consistency between $\boldsymbol{f}$ and $\boldsymbol{x}=\left(x_p^u\right)_{p\in P, u\in S_p\cup T_p}$ implies that for all arcs $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f})$
and all $s\in S_i$, it holds that $x_i^s=1$ (node $i$ receives flow only from source $s$) if $x_j^s=1$
(node $j$ receives flow only from source $s$).
Hence, $f_{ij}=0$ if $x_i^s=0$ and $x_j^s=1$, which is imposed by $f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\left(1-x_j^s+x_i^s\right)$.
A symmetric argument suggests the constraint $f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\left(1-x_i^t+x_j^t\right)$ for each $t\in T_j$.
A basic MILP model thus reads:
\begin{eqnarray}
\min & \sum_{(i,j)\in A}c_{ij}f_{ij} \label{eq:objMCCIFP} \\
     & \sum_{s\in S_p}x_p^s+\sum_{t\in T_p}x_p^t = 1 & p\in P \label{eq:convMCCIFP} \\
     & f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\left(1-x_j^s+x_i^s\right) & (i,j)\in A, s\in S_j \label{eq:sMCCIFP} \\
     & f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\left(1-x_i^t+x_j^t\right) & (i,j)\in A, t\in T_i \label{eq:tMCCIFP} \\
     & \boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b}) \label{eq:fMCCIFP} \\
     & x_p^u\in\{0,1\} & p\in P, u\in S_p\cup T_p \label{eq:xMCCIFP}
\end{eqnarray}

For arcs $(i,j)\in A$, where $i\in S$, \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP} is simplified to $f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}(1-x_j^s)$ for all $s\neq i$, since $x_i^s=0$.
Hence, $(i,j)$ does not carry flow if $j$ receives all its flow from some source $s\neq i$.
Likewise, \eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} implies no flow along arc $(i,j)$ if $j\in T$ and $x_i^t=1$ for some sink $t\in T_i\setminus\{j\}$.

That the feasible region of model \eqref{eq:objMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP} corresponds exactly to the weakly confluent-influent flows in $F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$ has a formal proof:
\begin{lemma} \label{lem:MCCIFP}
Let $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ be a feasible solution to \eqref{eq:objMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP}.
For every path $(s,p_1,\ldots,p_r,t)$ in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$, where $s\in S$ and $t\in T$,
there exists some integer $q\in\{0,1,\ldots,r\}$ such that $x_{p_k}^s=1$ for all $0\leq k\leq q$ and $x_{p_k}^t=1$ for all $q<k\leq r+1$, where $p_0=s$ and $p_{r+1}=t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider any $k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$.
By \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP} and \eqref{eq:xMCCIFP}, either $x_{p_k}^u=1$ for some $u\in S$ or $x_{p_k}^v=1$ for some $v\in T$.
In the first case, \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP} applied to $(i,j)=(p_{k-1},p_k)\in A$ yields $x_{p_{k-1}}^u=1$, since $(p_{k-1},p_k)\in A(\boldsymbol{f})$ implies $f_{p_{k-1}p_k}>0$.
By induction, it follows that $x_{p_0}^u=x_s^u=1$, which means that $s=u$.
In the second case, by \eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} applied to $(p_k,p_{k+1}),\ldots,(p_r,t)$, it holds that $x_t^v=1$ and $t=v$.
The claim then follows from $x_{p_0}^s=x_{p_{r+1}}^t=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:MCCIFP}
A vector $\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{R}^A$ is a weakly confluent-influent flow in $F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$ if and only if
$(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ satisfies \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP} for some $\boldsymbol{x}\in\{0,1\}^P$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If: Assume $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ satisfies \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP}.
% Consider a path $(u,p_1,\ldots,p_r,v)$ in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$, where $u\in S$ and $v\in T$.
% By \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP} and \eqref{eq:xMCCIFP}, either $x_{p_k}^s=1$ for some $s\in S$ or $x_{p_k}^t=1$ for some $t\in T$ ($k=1,\ldots,r$).
% In the first case, \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP} applied to $(i,j)=(p_{k-1},p_k)\in A$ implies $x_{p_{k-1}}^s=1$, since $f_{p_{k-1}p_k}>0$.
% By induction, it follows that $x_u^s=1$, which means that $s=u$.
% In the second case, by \eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} applied to $(p_k,p_{k+1}),\ldots,(p_r,v)$, it holds that $x_v^t=1$ and $t=v$.
Consider an arbitrary $p\in P\cap N(\boldsymbol{f})$.
If $x_p^s=1$ for some $s\in S$, Lemma~\ref{lem:MCCIFP} says that $x_u^s=1$ at all nodes $u$ on paths from $S$ to $p$ in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$.
Since $x_u^s=0$ at sources $u\neq s$, it follows that all paths to $p$ start from source $s$.
Otherwise, $x_p^t=1$ for some $t\in T$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:MCCIFP}, $x_u^t=1$ at all nodes $u$ on paths from $p$ to $T$, and $x_u^t=0$ at sinks $u\neq t$ proves that all paths terminate at sink $t$.
Hence, $\boldsymbol{f}$ is a weakly confluent-influent flow in $F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$.

Only if: Assume $\boldsymbol{f}$ is a weakly confluent-influent flow, which immediately implies \eqref{eq:fMCCIFP}.
For all $p\in P\setminus N(\boldsymbol{f})$ and $u\in S_p\cup T_p$, choose $x_p^u\in\{0,1\}$ arbitrarily such that \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP} is satisfied.
For arcs $(i,j)\in A\setminus A(\boldsymbol{f})$, $f_{ij}=0$ shows that \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} are satisfied.
Consider a $p\in P\cap N(\boldsymbol{f})$. If $p$ receives all its flow from a uniques source $s$, let $x_p^s=1$ and $x_p^u=0$ for all $u\in T_p\cup S_p\setminus\{s\}$.
Otherwise, $p$ sends all flow to a unique sink $t$ since $\boldsymbol{f}$ is weakly con fluent-influent.
In that case, let $x_p^t=1$ and $x_p^u=0$ for all $u\in S_p\cup T_p\setminus\{t\}$. Thus, \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP} is satisfied.
For any $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f})$, node $i$ receives all its flow from $s$ if node $j$ does.
Hence, $x_i^s=1$ if $x_j^s=1$. Analogously, $x_j^t=1$ if $x_i^t=1$.
Consequently, the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} is no less than $h_{ij}$,
and the inequalities hold since $f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary} \label{cor:MCCIFP}
For any feasible solution $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ to \eqref{eq:objMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP},
the arc set $\bar{A}(\boldsymbol{f})=\left\{(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f}):\sum_{s\in S_i}x_i^s=\sum_{t\in T_j}x_j^t=1\right\}$ is a cut set in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$ separating $S$ from $T$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Consider any $S-T$-path in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$.
The claim follows by observing that according to Lemma~\ref{lem:MCCIFP}, there exists an arc $(i,j)$ on the path such that $x_i^s=x_j^t=1$ for some $(s,t)\in(S_i\times T_j)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Multi-commodity flow formulations} \label{sec:mciMILP}
For all $(i,j)\in A$ and $(s,t)\in S_i\times T_j$, denote by $f_{ij}^{st}$ the flow from source $s$ along arc $(i,j)$ to sink $t$,
and let $f_{ij}^{st}=0$ for $(s,t)\in (S\times T)\setminus(S_i\times T_j)$.
An upper bound, $h_{ij}^{st}\leq h_{ij}$, on this flow is given as the maximum flow from $j$ to $i$ in the digraph $D_{ij}^{st}=(N,A_{ij}^{st})$,
where $A_{ij}^{st}=\left(A\cup\{(t,s)\}\right)\setminus\{(i,j)\}$ with capacity $h_{ts}=h_{ij}$ on arc $(t,s)$.
Let $\boldsymbol{f}^{st}=\left(f_{ij}^{st}\right)_{(i,j)\in A}$.

Recall the binary variables and constants $x_i^u$ ($i\in N$, $u\in S\cup T$) introduced in Section~\ref{sec:sciMILP},
along with the binary constants $x_u^v=1$ for $u=v$ and $x_u^v=0$ for $u\neq v$ ($u,v\in S\cup T$).
When transforming single- to multi-commodity flow variables, the proportions of the flow at node $p$ supplied at source $s$ and delivered at sink $t$ become useful.
For any $\boldsymbol{f}\in F(D,\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{b})$, let $w_u^v=x_u^v$ for all $u,v\in S\cup T$, and let
\begin{eqnarray}
w_p^s = \frac{\sum_{i\in N_p^-}w_i^sf_{ip}}{\sum_{i\in N_p^-}f_{ip}}, & ~p\in P\cap N(\boldsymbol{f}), s\in S_p \label{eq:ws} \\
w_p^t = \frac{\sum_{j\in N_p^+}w_j^tf_{pj}}{\sum_{j\in N_p^+}f_{pj}}, & ~p\in P\cap N(\boldsymbol{f}), t\in T_p. \label{eq:wt}
\end{eqnarray}

\begin{lemma} \label{lem:w}
For all $p\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$, $\sum_{s\in S_p}w_p^s=\sum_{t\in T_p}w_p^t=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $i\in S$, $\sum_{s\in S}w_i^s=1$ is obvious. 
Assume $\sum_{s\in S_i}w_i^s=1$ for all $i\in N_p^-(\boldsymbol{f})$, where $p\in P\cap N(\boldsymbol{f})$.
Then, by \eqref{eq:ws}, $\sum_{s\in S_p}w_p^s=\sum_{s\in S_p}\frac{\sum_{i\in N_p^-}w_i^sf_{ip}}{\sum_{i\in N_p^-}f_{ip}}=1$.
Because $D(\boldsymbol{f})$ is acyclic, induction proves $\sum_{s\in S_p}w_p^s=1$ for all $p\in N(\boldsymbol{f})$.
The proof of $\sum_{t\in T_p}w_p^t=1$ is analogous.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:multi}
Let $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ be a pair of vectors satisfying \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP} and \eqref{eq:fMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP}.
Then, inequalities \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} hold if and only if there exist flow vectors
$\boldsymbol{f}^{st}\in\mathbb{R}_+^A$ for all $(s,t)\in S\times T$ such that $\boldsymbol{f} = \sum_{(s,t)\in S\times T}\boldsymbol{f}^{st}$,
$\sum_{j\in N_i^-}f_{ji}^{st} = \sum_{j\in N_i^+}f_{ij}^{st}$ for all $i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})\setminus\{s,t\}$, and such that
$x_i^s+x_i^t=x_j^s+x_j^t=1\leq x_i^s+x_j^t$ for all $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f}^{st})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If: Assume that flow vectors $\boldsymbol{f}^{st}$ satisfying the said conditions exist. % and consider an arbitrary arc $(i,j)\in A$. %$(\boldsymbol{f})$
At arcs $(i,j)\notin A(\boldsymbol{f})$, it holds that $f_{ij}=0$ satisfies \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} since the right-hand side of both inequalities is non-negative for all $(s,t)\in S_i\times T_j$.
For arcs $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f})$, observe first that \eqref{eq:fMCCIFP} implies $f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}$.
Therefore, it suffices to prove $y_{ij}^s\geq 1$ and $y_{ij}^t\geq 1$ for all $(s,t)\in S_i\times T_j$, where $y_{ij}^s=1-x_j^s+x_i^s$ and $y_{ij}^t=1-x_i^t+x_j^t$.
From $\boldsymbol{f} = \sum_{(s,t)\in S\times T}\boldsymbol{f}^{st}$, it follows that $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f^{st}})$ for at least one $(s,t)\in S_i\times T_j$.
Because of the assumption $x_i^s+x_i^t=x_j^s+x_j^t=1$, \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP} implies $x_i^u=x_j^u=0$ for all $u\in(S_i\cup T_j)\setminus\{s,t\}$, implying $y_{ij}^s=y_{ij}^t=1$.
Further, combining \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP} and the assumption $x_i^s+x_j^t\geq 1$ yields $y_{ij}^s=1-x_j^s+x_i^s\geq x_j^t+x_i^s\geq 1$ and $y_{ij}^t=1-x_i^t+x_j^t\geq x_i^s+x_j^t\geq 1$. 
It follows that \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} hold for all $(s,t)\in S_i\times T_j$.

Only if: 
Assume that $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ satisfies \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP}.
For arcs $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f})$, define $f_{ij}^{st} = f_{ij}w_i^sw_j^t$ for all $(s,t)\in S_i\times T_j$ and $f_{ij}^{st}=0$ for all $(s,t)\in(S\times T)\setminus(S_i\times T_j)$.
Let $f_{ij}^{st}=0$ for $(i,j)\in A\setminus A(\boldsymbol{f})$.
Then, $\sum_{i\in N_p^-}f_{ip}^{st} = w_p^t\sum_{i\in N_p^-}w_i^sf_{ip} = w_p^tw_p^s\sum_{i\in N_p^-}f_{ip} = w_p^tw_p^s\sum_{j\in N_p^+}f_{pj} = w_p^s\sum_{j\in N_p^+}w_j^tf_{pj} = \sum_{j\in N_p^+}f_{pj}^{st}$.
Further, $\sum_{s\in S_i}\sum_{t\in T_j}f_{ij}^{st}=\sum_{s\in S_i}\sum_{t\in T_j}f_{ij}w_i^sw_j^t=f_{ij}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:w}.

Consider any $i,j)\in A$ and $(s,t)\in S_i\times T_j$ such that $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f}^{st})\subseteq A(\boldsymbol{f})$.
Since $\boldsymbol{f}^{st}$ satisfies conservation of flow at $P$, there exists a path in $D(\boldsymbol{f}^{st})$ from $s$ to $t$ intersecting $(i,j)$.
Because $\boldsymbol{f}^{st}\leq\boldsymbol{f}$, this is also a path in $D(\boldsymbol{f}^{st})$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:MCCIFP} then shows that for $k\in\{i,j\}$, either $x_k^s=1$ or $x_k^t=1$,
and that $x_i^s+x_j^t\geq 1$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:multi} suggests a multi-commodity flow formulation of the weak MCCIFP:
% Imposing $f_{ij}^{st}=0$ unless $x_i^s+x_i^t=x_j^s+x_j^t=1$ and $x_i^t+x_j^s\leq 1$, a multi-commodity flow formulation for the weak MCCIFP is given as:

\begin{eqnarray}
\min & \sum_{(i,j)\in A}c_{ij}f_{ij} \label{eq:objmMCCIFP} \\
     & f_{ij} = \sum_{s\in S_i}\sum_{t\in T_j}f_{ij}^{st}, & (i,j)\in A \label{eq:fmMCCIFP} \\
     & \sum_{j\in N_i^-}f_{ji}^{st} = \sum_{j\in N_i^+}f_{ij}^{st}, & i\in N(\boldsymbol{f})\setminus\{s,t\} \label{eq:stmMCCIFP} \\
     & 0\leq f_{ij}^{st}\leq h_{ij}^{st}\left(x_i^s+x_i^t\right), & (i,j)\in A, s\in S_j, t\in T_i \label{eq:istmMCCIFP} \\
     & 0\leq f_{ij}^{st}\leq h_{ij}^{st}\left(x_j^s+x_j^t\right), & (i,j)\in A, s\in S_j, t\in T_i \label{eq:jtsmMCCIFP} \\
     & 0\leq f_{ij}^{st}\leq h_{ij}^{st}\left(x_i^s+x_j^t\right), & (i,j)\in A, s\in S_j, t\in T_i \label{eq:isjtmMCCIFP} \\
     & \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP}-\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP}\label{eq:mMCCIFP} \label{eq:allMCCIFP}
\end{eqnarray}

Observe that by including, e.g., all $s\in S_j\setminus S_i$ in \eqref{eq:istmMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:isjtmMCCIFP}, $f_{ij}^{st}=0$ is imposed for sources $s$ that cannot support node $i$.
According to Proposition~\ref{prop:multi}, inequalities \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP} are redundant in the multi-commodity formulation \eqref{eq:objmMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:allMCCIFP}.

\section{Valid inequalities} \label{sec:vi}
\subsection{A strengthened single-commodity formulation} \label{sec:vis}
Redundant valid inequalities in the single-commodity model are derived by applying the idea behind constraints \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP}
to sets of sources or sinks.
Consider an arc $(i,j) \in A$. If $j$ receives all its flow from some source $s\in S'\subseteq S$, i.e., if $\sum_{s\in S'}x_j^s=1$,
then there is no flow along $(i,j)$ unless also $\sum_{s\in S'}x_i^s=1$.
Therefore, the inequalities
\begin{eqnarray}
 & f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\left(1+\sum_{s\in S'}(x_i^s-x_j^s)\right) & (i,j)\in A, S'\subseteq S \label{eq:visMCCIFP} \\
 & f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\left(1+\sum_{t\in T'}(x_j^t-x_i^t)\right) & (i,j)\in A, T'\subseteq T \label{eq:vitMCCIFP}
\end{eqnarray}
are valid.
Alternatively, \eqref{eq:visMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:vitMCCIFP} are derived from the observation that along any path $(i_1,\ldots,i_m)$ in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$,
$x_{i_k}^u$ is non-increasing with $k$ for all $u\in S$, and non-decreasing for all $u\in T$.
This follows directly from \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:tMCCIFP}.
The observation also implies validity of the combined inequality
\begin{equation}
	\displaystyle{f_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\left(1+\sum_{s\in S'}(x_i^s-x_j^s) + \sum_{t\in T'}(x_j^t-x_i^t)\right)}, ~(i,j)\in A, S'\subseteq S, T'\subseteq T, \label{eq:vistMCCIFP}
\end{equation}

\noindent
which is a generalization of \eqref{eq:visMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:vitMCCIFP}.
The potential of inequality \eqref{eq:visMCCIFP} to strengthen \eqref{eq:sMCCIFP}, and \eqref{eq:vitMCCIFP} to strengthen \eqref{eq:tMCCIFP}, are addressed next:
\begin{proposition} \label{prop:strong}
Let $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ be a feasible solution to the continuous relaxation of \eqref{eq:objMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP}.
If, for every arc $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f})$, it holds that $x_i^s\geq x_j^s$ for all $s\in S_j$ and $x_i^t\leq x_j^t$ for all $t\in T_i$,
then $\boldsymbol{f}$ is weakly confluent-influent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The proof is by contradiction.
Assume $\boldsymbol{f}$ is not weakly confluent-influent. Then, there exist a node $p\in P$,
distinct sources $s_0,s_1\in S_p$, distinct sinks $t_0,t_1\in T_p$, and paths $Q_{k\ell}$ ($k,\ell=0,1$) in $D(\boldsymbol{f})$,
where $Q_{k\ell}$ intersects $s_k$, $p$, and $t_{\ell}$.
By definition, $x_{s_k}^{s_{1-k}}=x_{t_k}^{t_{1-k}}=0$ ($k=0,1$).
As all arcs of $Q_{k\ell}$ are in $A(\boldsymbol{f})$, repeated application of $x_i^{s_1}\geq x_j^{s_1}$ to the arcs between $s_0$ and $p$ in $Q_{0\ell}$
proves $x_p^{s_1}=0$. Analogous operations along $Q_{1\ell}$ yield $x_p^{s_0}=0$.
Likewise, applying $x_i^{t_{1-\ell}}\leq x_j^{t_{1-\ell}}$ to the arcs between $t_{\ell}$ and $p$ in $Q_{k\ell}$ yields $x_p^{t_{\ell}}=0$ ($\ell=0,1$).
Hence, by \eqref{eq:convMCCIFP}, $x_p^u>0$ for some $u\in (S_p\cup T_p)\setminus\{s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1\}$.
If $u\in T_p$, applying $x_i^u\leq x_j^u$ to arcs $(i,j)$ along $Q_{k\ell}$ from $p$ to $t_{\ell}$ yields $x_{t_{\ell}}^u>0$, which is a contradiction.
An analogous analysis applies if $u\in S_p$.
\end{proof}

Consider an optimal solution $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$ to the continuous relaxation of \eqref{eq:objMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP},
and for all $(i,j)\in A$, let $\alpha_{ij}^s=x_i^s-x_j^s$ ($s\in S_j$) and $\alpha_{ij}^t=x_j^t-x_i^t$ ($t\in T_i$).
If $\boldsymbol{f}$ is weakly confluent-influent, then it is also an optimal flow in the MILP model.
Even if $\boldsymbol{x}$ is fractional, it can easily be adjusted to integer values consistent with $\boldsymbol{f}$.
Otherwise, Proposition \ref{prop:strong} shows that there exists some $(i,j)\in A(\boldsymbol{f})$ at which $\alpha_{ij}^u<0$ for some $u\in S_j\cup T_i$.
If there exist two distinct $u,v\in S_j\cup T_i$ such that $\alpha_{ij}^u,\alpha_{ij}^v<0$,
formulation \eqref{eq:objMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:xMCCIFP} is strengthened by adding the inequality \eqref{eq:vistMCCIFP} corresponding to $S'\cup T'=\{u,v\}$.
If $f_{ij}>h_{ij}\left(1+\alpha_{ij}^u+\alpha_{ij}^v\right)$, then the added inequality cuts off $(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{x})$.

\subsection{A strengthened multi-commodity formulation} \label{sec:vim}
The idea leading to the valid inequality \eqref{eq:vistMCCIFP} in the single-commodity flow formulation can also be applied to multi-commodity flow.
Let $h_{ij}^{S'T'}$ denote an upper bound on the flow from $S'$ via $(i,j)$ to $T'$ in $D$.
Then, \eqref{eq:istmMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:isjtmMCCIFP} are generalized by
\begin{equation}
     \sum_{(s,t)\in S'\times T'}f_{ij}^{st}\leq h_{ij}^{S'T'}\left(\sum_{s\in S'}x_i^s+\sum_{t\in T'}x_i^t\right),
     ~(i,j)\in A, \emptyset\neq S'\subseteq S_j, \emptyset\neq T'\subseteq T_i \label{eq:vistmMCCIFP}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
     \sum_{(s,t)\in S'\times T'}f_{ij}^{st}\leq h_{ij}^{S'T'}\left(\sum_{s\in S'}x_j^s+\sum_{t\in T'}x_j^t\right),
     ~(i,j)\in A, \emptyset\neq S'\subseteq S_j, \emptyset\neq T'\subseteq T_i \label{eq:vjtsmMCCIFP}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
     \sum_{(s,t)\in S'\times T'}f_{ij}^{st}\leq h_{ij}^{S'T'}\left(\sum_{s\in S'}x_i^s+\sum_{t\in T'}x_j^t\right),
     ~(i,j)\in A, \emptyset\neq S'\subseteq S_j, \emptyset\neq T'\subseteq T_i \label{eq:visjtMCCIFP}
\end{equation}

The potential of improved strength in \eqref{eq:vistmMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:visjtMCCIFP} over \eqref{eq:istmMCCIFP}--\eqref{eq:isjtmMCCIFP} lies in the feature that, e.g.,
\eqref{eq:vistmMCCIFP} is not necessarily a sum of the constraints \eqref{eq:istmMCCIFP} corresponding to $(s,t)\in S'\times T'$.
For instance, if $S'=\{s_1,s_2\}$, $T'=\{t\}$, and $h_{ij}^{S'T'}=h_{ij}^{s_1t}=h_{ij}^{s_2t}=h_{ij}$, then summing the corresponding two inequalities \eqref{eq:istmMCCIFP} yields
\[
f_{ij}^{s_1t}+f_{ij}^{s_2t}\leq h_{ij}(x_i^{s_1}+x_i^t) + h_{ij}(x_i^{s_2}+x_i^t),
\]
\noindent
whereas \eqref{eq:vistmMCCIFP} reads
\[
f_{ij}^{s_1t}+f_{ij}^{s_2t}\leq h_{ij}(x_i^{s_1}+x_i^{s_2}+x_i^t),
\]
\noindent
which obviously is stronger.
